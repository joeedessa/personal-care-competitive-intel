Category spread — how much premium each category tolerates

What it measures. Within one category, on a like-for-like basis, how many times the cheapest option the dearest one costs. Body lotion at 41× means the most expensive body lotion tracked costs 41 times the cheapest per 100 ml.

How to read it. A wide spread means customers in that category already accept an enormous premium for brand — the ceiling is high and there is room to price up. A narrow spread means price is compressed and everyone is clustered: you would be competing on distribution, design or story rather than on where you sit on the ladder.

The caveat. Spread is driven by its endpoints, so a single mass benchmark at the bottom stretches it. The named brands at each end tell you whether the number is real or an artefact — filter to Luxury + Premium to see the spread among genuine competitors.

Newsroom

Every headline collected across the tracked brand set, newest first — brand coverage, trade press and topic queries. Refreshed daily. Deduped by title; a brand tag means the headline surfaced against that brand's query, not that the brand is the subject.

What brands are doing

Headlines auto-tagged into initiative buckets by keyword. A shape-of-activity read, not a precise count — tune the rules in data/feeds.json.

Share of voice

Volume of coverage picked up per brand. Volume is not sentiment — read it as who is generating news, not who is winning.

Coverage over time

Headlines collected per week. Read the shape, not the slope. The line rises toward the present because news search returns far denser coverage for recent weeks than for older ones — that is the collector, not the market. Week-on-week spikes within the recent window are meaningful; the overall upward drift is not.

Who is gaining share of voice

Each brand's share of all coverage in the last 30 days against its share in the 30 before — share, not raw count, precisely because raw counts rise for everyone as the window approaches today. A brand needs a real prior baseline to be called a riser; brands appearing for the first time are listed separately, because a thin archive cannot tell the difference between "new" and "previously missed".

Initiative matrix

Which brands are active on which fronts. A brand heavy on retail expansion and light on sustainability is telling you where its money is going this year. Empty columns across the whole set mark a theme nobody has claimed.

Emerging & scaling brands

Brands outside the established set that are taking share, setting price, or inventing a mechanic worth copying. Momentum is a judgement call (1–5), not a measured figure — it exists to force a ranking, and should be revised whenever the signals change.
